Academic Background, Mission & Vision

I am a Computer, Data, and Information Scientist and Educator, currently serving as an Assistant Professor of Computer Science at Texas Woman’s University (TWU) and the Principal Investigator on an active $600K collaborative National Science Foundation (NSF) grant. My academic trajectory was shaped by systematic, rigorous training in Mathematics, Systems Engineering, and Computer & Information Science across Egypt and leading United States institutions. It was further refined through doctoral and postdoctoral research at the University of Texas at Austin (UT Austin) and the University of Arkansas at Little Rock (UA Little Rock), where I earned my Ph.D. in Computer and Information Science, and anchored by enterprise industry experience at global technology leaders including Intel and AbbVie.

I am dedicated to advancing leadership in critical and emerging technologies. My work at the intersection of AI and the Life Sciences addresses high-priority goals in public health, computational biosecurity, and biomedical foundation models. By developing transparent, interpretable, and ethical AI frameworks, I provide the foundational computational tools necessary to collaborate and lead in global biotechnology.

Information Science fundamentally unites computer science, data science, library science, psychology, and cognitive ergonomics. Originating from an Electrical and Computer Engineering background, I was drawn to this interdisciplinary synthesis to understand how complex data architectures interact with real-world users and biomedical discovery pipelines. My doctoral and ongoing investigations bridge automated data integration, entity resolution, and knowledge discovery—uniting graph theory, machine learning, and natural language processing to resolve ambiguity, optimize modularity, and extract truth from complex, disparate data sources.

My research group investigates data quality within foundation models, specifically as it applies to proteomics, structural biology, drug discovery, and biomedical knowledge discovery. Our multimodal neuro-symbolic methodology harmonizes classical statistical rigor (Bayesian frameworks, Markovian transition matrices) with contemporary architectures like Graph Neural Networks and Transformers to ensure both high predictive accuracy and physical interpretability.

My career is anchored in service, integrity, and inclusive mentorship. My commitment to academic excellence ensures that the next generation of scientists is equipped to solve the most complex computational challenges of the 21st century. I mentor emerging researchers through a collaborative, guild-based apprenticeship model that prioritizes technical mastery and underrepresented voices in computational science. Beyond the laboratory, I contribute to community-building initiatives and public discourse on national, international, and cyber security, while providing technical advisory across end-to-end data science lifecycles. I find balance through civic, social, and spiritual engagement—whether through community leadership, Islamic scholarship, hiking, creative writing, or visiting historical archives.

Currently, I am working on a new theoretical AI architecture to bridge the gap in my publications, ensuring my research contributions span both fundamental theory and applied computational sciences.

Mission

To advance scientific discovery by fostering an accessible, ethical, and artificially intelligent data ecosystem. Driving automatic integration of scientific information, ensuring responsible and sustainable scientific computing, and empowering underrepresented groups in computational and data sciences through collaborative guild mentorship.

Vision

A future where data quality, graph theory, and foundation models unite into transparent, ethical systems that accelerate biomedical discovery. To define the next generation of biomedical intelligence where structured relational networks clarify the underlying logic of life.

Current Research

Proteomics & GCN

ProtGram-DirectGCN

A novel method for protein-protein interaction (PPI) prediction using directed graph convolutional neural networks. This work infers global dense residue transition graphs directly from primary sequence tokens, bypassing computationally heavy 3D folding while achieving state-of-the-art predictive performance.
